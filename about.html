A little about me

Hi. I’m Nadya, and I’m an old engineer.

Not that old. I never wore oversized sweaters or grew a proper programmer beard.

But I’ve been around long enough to remember when software came on floppy disks. Yes, those things you still see in Save icons.

I used Norton Commander. I copied music onto hard drives. I optimized programs because they simply wouldn’t fit on a floppy disk. I downloaded books from the internet one painfully slow page at a time.

Over the years, I’ve built backend software for large companies, engineering and CAD software, and mobile apps, including software for some of the very first Android devices.

I’ve used a ridiculous number of languages along the way: C, Pascal, Object Pascal, C++, C#, Java, Python, Swift, Kotlin… and quite a few others I’ve probably forgotten. I’ve worked with hundreds of frameworks, tools, platforms, and environments.

I first tried Android when it was basically brand new, around version 1.1. Since then, I’ve watched the platform, the devices, the tools, the languages, and the whole industry change over and over again.

And that’s probably the thing I like most about software.

It’s never finished.

There is always something new to learn, something weird to figure out, or some completely new way of building things. After all these years, I still get genuinely excited when I discover something I haven’t seen before.

For a long time, I thought writing about engineering was something other people did. You know, those very serious, very knowledgeable programmers with the impressive beards who clearly knew everything already.

Eventually I decided to give it a try anyway.

So this is me experimenting in public.

I’ll write about things I build, things I break, things I learn, and rabbit holes I happen to fall into. Some of it will be useful. Some of it might be obvious. Some of it will probably be wrong.

If you enjoy it, let me know. If you don’t, well… you don’t have to read it.

Either way, welcome.
